Stem Cells for COPD: How Does It Work?

Published: May 9, 2025

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD)

COPD is an umbrella term for chronic lung diseases causing airflow limitation - not one disease.

COPD is a leading global killer - about 5% of all deaths, third-ranked by 2023. China has nearly 100 million patients!

Conventional care includes long-term low-flow oxygen over 15 hours daily, plus bronchodilators (beta-2 agonists, cholinergic blockers, methylxanthines)

These ease symptoms and prevent exacerbations but cannot halt progression or repair lung tissue - nor lower mortality.

Clinical progress of stem cells for COPD

In 2019 China registered the experimental study of autologous bronchial basal cell transplant for COPD.

Numerous studies show stem cell therapy is safe and effective - quickly relieving or halting dyspnea in severe and critical patients.

Many COPD stem cell trials are underway in recent years.

As early as 2008, the world's first stem cell drug Prochymal entered a Phase II multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial for moderate-to-severe COPD.

No serious adverse events occurred; MSC therapy significantly lowered C-reactive protein and improved clinical presentation - preliminarily confirming safety and efficacy.

Since then, trials have registered in growing numbers on clinicaltrials.gov

As of May 24, 2022, ClinicalTrials.gov listed 33 stem-cell-based COPD trials, 12 ongoing.

A July 2012 review in the Journal of Traditional Chinese Medicine summarized published MSC-for-COPD clinical results

In May 2022, Cell published a systematic review and meta-analysis of stem-cell-based regenerative therapies and derivatives in COPD.

It found treated patients' FEV1 rose significantly - an average 71 ml over baseline.

Overall, the study provides valuable evidence that stem-cell-based treatments and products bring substantive benefit to COPD patients.

In August 2023, Cureus published 'Stem cell therapy in COPD: an in-depth look'.

1. Tissue regeneration and repair: differentiation into lung epithelial and endothelial cells offers structural repair;

2. Immune control: MSCs tune responses, limit excess inflammation and foster an anti-inflammatory environment;

3. Anti-fibrosis: ability to counter pulmonary fibrosis and reduce its extent;

4. Enhanced vascular function: promoting angiogenesis to improve blood supply;

5. Paracrine action: releasing cytokines, growth factors, chemokines and extracellular vesicles that promote repair;

6. Anti-inflammation and immune modulation: regulating immune-cell activity and releasing IL-10, TGF-beta and PGE2 to further ease chronic inflammation.
