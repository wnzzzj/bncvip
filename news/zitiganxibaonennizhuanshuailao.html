Nature Finding: The Age-70 'Cliff' May Stem from Hematopoietic Stem Cell Mutations

Published: April 28, 2025

The bone marrow's autologous hematopoietic stem cell bank produces about 17 million blood cells every second.

From birth we age with the years - an apparently irresistible law of nature.

Age is a natural marker with biological roots. The ancients called seventy rare; today it is common.

Some experts consider 'blood aging' one root cause

In 2022 Cambridge researchers reported in Nature their analysis of elderly blood: hematopoietic stem cells slowly accumulate mutations throughout life, and after 70

Studying blood cells from newborns to 81-year-olds, the team found adults under 70 derive blood from 20,000-200,000 stem cells contributing roughly equally

Why? The team explains:

Though blood-making stem cells accumulate mutations with time - most harmless - the problem comes when rare driver mutations let mutant cells outgrow

At 30-40 such mutants hold almost no growth edge; past 70 they dominate the marrow and command blood production

A key observation: some mutations make the abnormal cells hyper-competitive - directly outcompeting normal ones

'Beyond natural aging, chronic inflammation, smoking, infection and chemotherapy may produce driver-mutation abnormal cells,' noted co-corresponding author Elisa Laurenti.

'Fighting aging for immortality' seems an eternal research theme - emperors once sought elixirs; today's scientists devote decades to autologous stem cell science

Since 1994, BNC chief scientist Dr. Lin Xiongbin has focused on non-transgenic autologous stem cell production and translation, including hematopoietic cells

For over 20 years BNC has combined enterprise operation with academic translation - holding to scientific value and human care, with successful cases benefiting clients and growing public trust
