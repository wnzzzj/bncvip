For Neurological Diseases, Stem Cells Are the Most Promising Therapy

Published: May 9, 2025

Preface: neurological diseases are complex and mostly hard to cure - a long-standing challenge. In recent years stem cell technology has infused new vitality: repairing and regenerating damaged tissue while modulating immunity

In the body, most tissues have some repair potential - the nervous system differs.

With the CNS's limited regeneration, neural damage is usually hard to reverse - multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's, spinal cord injury and traumatic brain injury are extremely difficult to treat.

Scientists are pursuing innovative therapies to raise treatment potential and bring patients more hope and recovery.

Stem-cell-based regenerative medicine is becoming highly attractive for these diseases; MSCs' unique traits make them a favorite cell source.

Studies show the mechanisms: paracrine neurotrophic and immune-modulating action, or regenerating and replacing damaged cells

Hundreds of millions troubled worldwide:

hard-to-cure neurological disease

As a key control system - transmitting information, directing movement, coordinating physiology - the nervous system's diseases, from genetics, environment, trauma or unknown causes, bring great suffering to patients and families

Diagram: localization diagnosis of neurological disease

Neurological disease spans neurodegeneration, conduction disorders and neuroinflammation, classified as:

- Vascular: cerebral infarction, hemorrhage, transient ischemic attack;

- CNS infection: encephalitis, meningitis, myelitis;

- Degenerative: Alzheimer's, Parkinson's;

- Trauma: subdural hematoma, contusions;

- Tumors: primary such as glioma, plus metastases;

- Demyelinating: multiple sclerosis, neuromyelitis optica;

- Metabolic/nutritional: B1-deficiency neuropathy, B12-deficiency subacute combined degeneration;

- Toxic and genetic: hepatolenticular degeneration and more.

Though varied, the main symptoms overlap: consciousness, movement, speech, cognition and mood disorders.

Etiologically, they fall into three types:

1. Neuron loss in specific regions - Parkinson's, multiple sclerosis;

2. Neuron loss after acute injury - stroke;

3. Impaired cell function - epilepsy.

Causes are usually multifactorial: aging, environment, chronic stress, TBI and gene mutations (APP, presenilin-1/-2, ApoE)

Today these diseases are a giant challenge: hard to treat, most patients receive no effective standardized therapy after diagnosis - the world's second-largest cause of death

With continuing exploration, understanding and treatment keep advancing.

Clinical application of stem cells for neurological disease

Among treatments, self-renewing multi-lineage stem cells are ideal for neural regeneration; recent trials show them a promising option for refractory disease.

Main mechanisms:

1. Repair: migrating to injury, replacing dead or damaged neurons and repairing networks;

2. Migration: after injury, MSCs home to the damaged microenvironment to maintain or remodel function, guided by cytokines and signaling;

3. Differentiation: secreting neurotrophic growth factors that activate neurons and drive regeneration, plus angiogenic factors for local vessels;

4. Immune modulation: inhibiting T-cell proliferation, balancing Th2/Th1, blocking the B-cell cycle and antibody production - anti-inflammatory protection.

Over 200 stem cell trials for neurological disease are registered - mainly MS, stroke, Parkinson's and spinal cord injury.

1. Stem cells and stroke (CVA)

Stroke causes neuronal damage; a STEM CELLS TRANSLATIONAL MEDICINE study shows stem cells regenerate damaged neurons, easing sequelae

Imaging of nine patients shows clear tissue change in lesion areas

Imaging clearly shows transplanted cells in infarct areas differentiating into neural cells, secreting trophic factors, promoting neural and vascular regeneration, repairing the blood-brain barrier and easing inflammation

In vessel remodeling and flow maintenance, paracrine VEGF promotes angiogenesis, restores endothelial integrity, reduces permeability and apoptosis.

2. Stem cells and Alzheimer's (AD)

Stem cells also bring new options for neurodegenerative patients.

Advanced Science reports hUC-MSCs repair damaged neurons, regulating tau phosphorylation via the HGF-cMet-AKT-GSK3beta pathway

Numerous trials show transplant not only calms cerebral inflammation but promotes neurogenesis and synaptic connection - effective and safe.

3. Stem cells and Parkinson's (PD)

Parkinson's is progressive neurodegeneration, mostly over 60, marked by neuronal death in many regions - stem cell therapy brings new breakthroughs.

To improve post-transplant survival, Harvard co-transplanted regulatory T cells with iPSC-derived dopaminergic neurons: survival rose sharply with rapid behavioral recovery

Autologous Treg combined cell transplant for Parkinson's

Bayer's stem cell drug BRT-DA01 reached its Phase I endpoint in August 2023 with encouraging results

By MDS-UPDRS Part III and Hauser Diary, all subjects improved on secondary endpoints - high-dose subjects most

Phase II is planned with recruitment from H1 2024 - new options and hope may arrive soon.

4. Stem cells and ALS

Against degenerative disease humanity was nearly helpless - but stem cells bring hope. ALS, nearly untreatable, sees new possibility through iPSCs

A JNeurosci study used iPSCs to find a possible gene variant in ALS patients - a potential new target

SYNGAP1 3'UTR variants in ALS recruit HNRNPK, causing aberrant splicing and dendritic spine loss

Another clinical study showed slowed progression in MSC-treated ALS patients, as in animal models.

A case-control study of 67 ALS patients given three intrathecal WJ-MSC infusions: 31% showed slowed progression

5. Stem cells and multiple sclerosis (MS)

Long called 'the cancer that doesn't kill', MS finds new salvation in stem cells.

In a primate MS model, intrathecal MSCs delayed neurologic dysfunction and demyelination.

Trials prove IT or IV MSC transplant feasible, safe and tolerable, with some patients stable or improved on EDSS.

A Swedish Phase I study of seven patients: remission-phase autologous BM-MSCs stabilized disability in 86%.

6. Stem cells and spinocerebellar ataxia (SCA)

SCA is another incurable neurodegeneration; studies used IV and intrathecal MSCs to assess safety and feasibility.

At 12 months: no serious adverse events, symptoms eased - a new strategy for SCA and genetic neural disease.

7. Stem cells and traumatic brain injury (TBI)

TBI occurs when sudden blows or shakes damage the brain; concussion is the most common type.

Through neuroplasticity - the lifelong forming of new connections - stem cell therapy stimulates neurons, creating new cells while reinforcing plasticity itself.

CANR and IANR China released the 2022 Clinical Neurorestoration Guidelines for TBI, listing cell-therapy trials as an option for moderate-severe injury.

The 2022 guidelines

An International Journal of Oral Science article revealed deep molecular mechanisms: easing neuroinflammation and repairing injury

Numerous and mostly incurable, neurological diseases meet in stem cells an innovative therapy - tissue regeneration and immune modulation at the cellular-genetic level

As regenerative medicine renews itself, slow-moving refractory diseases enter an era of great strides - stem cells promise new options for more patients.
