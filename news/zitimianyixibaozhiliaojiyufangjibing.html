Autologous NK Cell Therapy: Anti-Cancer and Anti-Aging in One

Published: January 28, 2026

The liver - the body's silently working chemical plant - has limited self-repair once damaged, and conventional treatment often hits bottlenecks. Stem cell therapy is becoming the frontier choice to break through, bringing new hope for liver repair through multiple synergistic mechanisms.

01. The silence of cirrhosis

Cirrhosis is a chronic progressive liver disease - essentially abnormal repair after long-term injury. As viral hepatitis, alcoholic or fatty liver disease persist, the liver launches excessive fibrosis; normal cells are replaced by scar tissue, finally forming a hard, nodular organ. The process is usually irreversible, with these features:

Functional impairment: detoxification, metabolism and synthesis (e.g. clotting factors) decline markedly, causing jaundice, ascites and coagulation problems.

Complication risk: portal hypertension can rupture esophageal varices (major bleeding), cause hepatic encephalopathy, even liver cancer.

Silent progression: early compensation is strong, with only vague fatigue or poor appetite easily overlooked; most patients are already decompensated at diagnosis, when treatment is far harder.

02. The limits of conventional treatment: delay, not reversal

Etiological treatment: antivirals, abstinence and metabolic control slow the course but cannot reverse fibrosis.

Symptomatic treatment: diuretics for ascites or endoscopic hemostasis for varices address complications only, not liver structure.

Transplant dilemma: the ultimate answer for end-stage patients, but hampered by donor shortage, huge cost (hundreds of thousands of yuan), rejection and infection risks.

03. Autologous stem cell therapy: recoding liver regeneration

For liver injury, BNC offers hematopoietic, mesenchymal and liver stem cell care plans, meeting personalized needs.

The core idea is transplanting regenerative stem cells to repair damaged tissue, restrain fibrosis and restore function, through several mechanisms:

Direct differentiation: guided by the injured microenvironment, stem cells settle and become functional hepatocytes, directly replenishing lost cells.

Paracrine effect: secreted growth factors and cytokines tune local immunity, ease inflammation and activate endogenous proliferation.

Anti-fibrosis: secreted matrix metalloproteinases degrade over-deposited matrix, slowing or even reversing fibrosis.

Immune modulation: stem cells restrain excessive immune attack on the liver, creating favorable conditions for repair.

Together these mechanisms let stem cells both replenish cells and improve the environment - systematic repair from structure to function.

04. Clinical breakthrough: from bench to bedside

Stem cells show potential to reverse injury, improve function and bring hope to patients unresponsive to conventional therapy

On January 6, 2025, teams from Penn's Perelman School of Medicine and Taipei Medical University published a review in Stem Cell Research & Therapy: 'Mesenchymal stem cell therapy may reshape liver disease treatment: a review of current trials'.

The review explores the latest MSC-based progress for chronic and end-stage liver disease in current trials.

Stem cells show potential in reversing injury and improving function, bringing hope where conventional therapy fails.

Delivered via hepatic, portal or peripheral veins, MSCs significantly improve histology, reduce fibrosis and restore function.

A PLA Fifth Medical Center review affirms good safety for stem cell liver therapy

The infection department of Hospital 301's Fifth Medical Center published 'Stem cell therapy for liver disease: current progress and future goals' in Hepatology International.

As of March 31, 2025, over 110 clinical trials of stem cell therapy for chronic liver disease were registered, mostly Phase I-II, showing good safety; emerging evidence also highlights initial efficacy in survival and functional recovery.

05. From irreversible to repairable

Stem cell therapy is ushering cirrhosis care into a new era. As Nobel laureate Shinya Yamanaka said, 'Stem cells are not magic - they are science.' With each technological iteration we approach the dream of reversing cirrhosis, bringing patients hope of full repair, structure and function alike.
